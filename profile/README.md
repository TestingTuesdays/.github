# 🧪 Testing Tuesdays

### Working products. Real users. Better releases, every Tuesday.

**Testing Tuesdays** is a Nairobi-based, community-driven product-testing program where founders bring functional products, watch people try real tasks, learn from structured feedback, and return with improvements to test again.

**Less pitching. More testing. More shipping.**

We are building a practical feedback loop between **startup founders, real users, product managers, designers, QA engineers, accessibility reviewers, and builders**.

> **Pilot status (November 2026):** Testing Tuesdays Nairobi is being prepared for an initial cohort. Dates, venue, participation details, and registration will be announced only after confirmation. **There is no approved public registration link yet.**

## Why Testing Tuesdays?

Building a product is only half the work. Understanding how people actually use it is what helps teams make it better.

Too often, early-stage teams receive compliments instead of actionable feedback, ship features without observing users, or struggle to find people who match their target audience.

Testing Tuesdays creates space to:
- **Test working products, not just ideas.**
- **Observe real behavior, not only opinions.**
- **Learn from peers** by testing someone else's product as well as your own.
- **Find usability, onboarding, accessibility, and quality issues** before they become bigger problems.
- **Turn findings into fixes**, then retest to see what actually changed.
- **Build lasting connections** among founders, users, researchers, designers, and QA practitioners.

## How it works

```text
Bring a working product
        ↓
Define one real user task
        ↓
Test with peers and, where available, matched users
        ↓
Record observations and actionable findings
        ↓
Choose a fix and ship an improvement
        ↓
Return, retest, and learn
```

### 1. Bring something people can use
Founders arrive with a functional MVP, beta, live product, or safely testable hardware workflow. A clear task and target user matter more than polished slides.

### 2. Give testing to get testing
The program is **contribution-first**. Founders seeking a testing slot are expected to complete a useful, reviewed test for another team first, unless organizers explicitly approve an exception. Referrals are welcome but do not guarantee admission.

### 3. Watch what happens
Participants try tasks with minimal coaching. Facilitators and reviewers help distinguish what users actually did from assumptions about why they did it.

### 4. Turn observations into action
Teams leave with prioritized findings and a concrete next step—not just a collection of opinions.

### 5. Come back with a better version
At a subsequent session, founders can show what changed and retest the original workflow. Progress is demonstrated through evidence, not just a demo or a closed issue.

## Who is it for?

| **Founder / startup team** | A real, testable product and willingness to test peers | Direct feedback, actionable findings, and a repeatable improvement cycle |
| **Product user / tester** | Curiosity and, where relevant, experience matching a product's intended audience | A voice in how emerging products are built |
| **Product manager / UX researcher** | Facilitation, discovery, and synthesis skills | Hands-on research practice and a stronger product community |

**You do not need to be a founder to contribute.** Independent testers and practitioners are an important part of the program.

## What kinds of products can participate?

We welcome **working MVPs, functional private betas, live products, and safely testable hardware** from different sectors. Revenue or an established user base is not required.

A product should have:
- A functional experience that another person can actually use.
- One clearly defined task and an idea of who the intended user is.
- A safe test environment (such as demo accounts and sample data).
- A founder willing to receive feedback, contribute to peer testing, and follow up.

**Pitch decks, static mockups, and landing pages without a functional task do not qualify for a founder product-testing seat.** They may be suitable for other community activities.

Participation is **curated**: expressing interest, getting referred, or attending another event does not automatically reserve a product-testing slot.

## What a session looks like

A typical session is designed around:

1. **Introductions and a short product spotlight** — what is being tested and for whom.
2. **Hands-on testing** — founders and participants try real tasks, individually or in facilitated groups.
3. **Structured feedback** — observations, reproducible issues, accessibility concerns, and opportunities.
4. **Prioritization** — one important change to make and a plan to revisit it.
5. **Follow-up and retesting** — return with evidence of what improved, what did not, and what was learned.

The initial Nairobi pilot is being designed as a **multi-session cohort**, so the focus stays on continuous improvement rather than a one-off demo day. The exact format may evolve with the community.

## Our principles

**Build in public where appropriate; protect private testing data.** We celebrate learning and share consented, aggregate outcomes. We do not publish private founder research, participant identities, credentials, recordings, or raw feedback without permission.

**Give before you get.** Useful, respectful feedback is a contribution.

**Real users matter.** Fellow founders provide valuable perspectives, but they do not replace people who actually match a product's intended users.

**Test accessibly and inclusively.** Different devices, abilities, experiences, and contexts can reveal different problems.

**Evidence beats hype.** We value observed user behavior, verified fixes, and honest learning—not invented metrics or testimonials.

**No pay-to-play testing priority.** Sponsorship does not buy admission, favorable research results, or private participant information.
